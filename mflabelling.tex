  \section{Introduction}
Let $\calG$ be a class of graphs and let $f:\NN\to\NN$ be a function.
We say that $\calG$ admits an \defin{$f(n)$-bit adjacency labelling scheme} if there exists a function $A:(\set{0,1}^*)^2\to\set{0,1}$
% , called an \defin{adjacency tester,}
such that for all positive integers $n$, for every $n$-vertex graph $G\in\calG$, there exists a function $\ell: V(G)\to\set{0,1}^*$ such that
$|\ell(v)|\leq f(n)$ for each vertex $v$ in $G$, and such that
for every two vertices $u$, $v$ in $G$,
\[
A(\ell(u),\ell(v))=\begin{cases}
0&\textrm{if $uv\notin E(G)$},\\
1&\textrm{if $uv\in E(G)$}.
\end{cases}
\]

\subsection{State of the art}
\label{ssec:state-of-art}
Adjacency labeling schemes were introduced by Kannan, Naor, and Rudich~\cite{kannan.naor.ea:implicit0,kannan.naor.ea:implicit} and independently by Muller~\cite{muller:local} in his PhD thesis, both in the late 1980s.
Since this initial work, adjacency labelling schemes and, more generally, informative labelling schemes have remained a very active area of research.
Here we review results most relevant to the current work, namely results on trees, bounded treewidth graphs, and planar
graphs.

Let us start with a simple example to better grasp the notion of the labelling scheme.
%Consider the class of all forests.
Given an $n$-vertex forest $F$,
for each component of $F$ fix an arbitrary vertex to be a root of $F$, and assign each vertex of $F$ with an identifier, i.e.\ a number in $\set{0,\ldots,n-1}$.
For each nonroot vertex $v$ of $F$,
let the label of $v$ be
the pair consisting of its identifier and the identifier of its parent.
For each root vertex $v$ of $F$,
let the label of $v$ be
the pair consisting of its identifier on both coordinates.
Clearly, given two labels of vertices in $F$,
a test of adjacency is simply to verify whether an identifier of one vertex is equal to a stored identifier of the parent of the other vertex.
This constitutes a $\lceil\log(n^2)\rceil=\lceil2\log n\rceil$-bit adjacency labelling scheme for forests.
There is a fascinating series of results improving on this simple scheme for forests. Chung~\cite{chung:universal} gave an adjacency labelling scheme for $n$-vertex forests with each label of length $\log n +\Oh(\log\log n)$.
Alstrup and Rauhe~\cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved} devised a scheme with labels of length $\log n+\Oh(\log^*n)$.
Finally, Alstrup, Dahlgaard, and Knudsen~\cite{alstrup.dahlgaard.ea:optimal} gave a $(\log n + \Oh(1))$-bit adjacency labelling scheme for forests, which is optimal up to the $\Oh(1)$ term.
Indeed, consider an $n$-vertex graph with no two vertices of the same neighborhood (e.g. a path for $n\geq4$).
We conclude that, in any adjacency labelling scheme,
the labels of the vertices must be all different and therefore one label must be of length at least $\lceil\log n\rceil$.

Gavoille and Labourel \cite{gavoille.labourel:shorter} presented
a $(1+o(1))\log n$-bit adjacency labelling scheme for graphs of bounded treewidth.
This is particularly relevant to the topic of this paper because of the following famous application of the Graph Minor Structure Theorem:
DeVos, Ding, Oporowski, Sanders, Reed, Seymour, and Vertigan~\cite{devos2004} showed that every graph
in a proper minor-closed class can be edge $2$-coloured so that each monochromatic subgraph
has bounded treewidth.
Therefore, given a proper minor-closed class of graphs $\calG$,
for each $G$ in $\calG$, we can fix such a $2$-coloring of edges of $G$, say with red and blue.
Now for each $G$ in $\calG$, each vertex of $G$ gets a label which is a concatenatenation of two labels given by the adjacency labelling schemes for the red subgraph of $G$ and for the blue subgraph of $G$. Thus, all labels are of length $(2+o(1))\log n$.
Given two labels of vertices in $G$,
a test of adjacency is simply to test whether there is a blue edge and whether there is a red edge.
This gives a $(2+o(1))\log n$-bit adjacency labelling scheme for a fixed proper minor-closed class of graphs, which remained the best-known scheme up to the present work.

Planar graphs are a prominent example of a proper minor-closed class of graphs and there is a long history of research
on their adjacency labelling schemes.
Since planar graphs are $5$-degenerate,
they admit a simple $\lceil6\log n\rceil$-bit adjacency labelling scheme, which was already observed by Muller~\cite{muller:local}.
Kannan, Naor, and Rudich~\cite{kannan.naor.ea:implicit} use a similar approach that makes use of the fact that planar graphs have arboricity 3 (so their edges can be partitioned into three forests \cite{nash-williams:edge-disjoint}) to devise an adjacency labelling scheme for planar graphs whose labels have length at most $\lceil4\log n\rceil$.
Of course, the later result of Gavoille and Labourel~\cite{gavoille.labourel:shorter} also applies and gives a scheme with labels of length $(2+o(1))\log n$.
The most recent breakthrough came with the application of the product structure theorem for planar graphs by Dujmovi\'c, Joret, Micek, Morin, Ueckerdt, and Wood~\cite{dujmovic.joret.ea:planar}.
Bonamy, Gavoille, and Pilipczuk~\cite{BGP22} recognized that the product structure is particularly useful for adjacency labelling scheme and broke the barrier of $(2+o(1))\log n$ introducing a scheme with labels of length $(\frac{4}{3}+o(1))\log n$.
Finally, Dujmovi\'c, Esperet, Joret, Micek, and Morin~\cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} also based on the product structure theorem gave a $(1+o(1))\log n$-bit adjacency labelling scheme for planar graphs, which is optimal up to the lower order term.

The proof of Dujmovi\'c~\emph{et~al.}~\cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} works for  graphs embeddable on any fixed surface, and generally for any proper minor-closed class excluding a fixed apex graph. Here a graph $X$ is \defin{apex} if $X-a$ is planar for some vertex $a\in V(X)$ (or $V(X)=\emptyset$). Such apex-minor-free classes are the limit of this approach (since a minor-closed class $\mathcal{G}$ has `product structure' if and only if some apex graph is not in $\mathcal{G}$). For example, prior to the present work, the best result for $K_6$-minor-free graphs was the $(2+o(1))\log n$-bit adjacency labelling scheme of Gavoille and Labourel~\cite{gavoille.labourel:shorter}.

  \section{Building blocks}

Let \defin{$\NN$} be the set of all nonnegative integers and let \defin{$\NN^+$} be the set of all positive integers.

  In this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, and undirected.  For a graph $G$, \defin{$V(G)$} denotes the vertex set of $G$, \defin{$E(G)$} denotes the edge set of $G$, and \defin{$K(G)$} denotes the set of cliques in $G$, where a \defin{clique} is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. For standard graph theory terms, we refer the reader to the textbook by \citet{diestel:graph}.


  Let $T$ be a rooted tree and let $r$ be the root of $T$. For each $x\in V(T)$, we define \defin{$P_T(x)$} to be the path in $T$ from $x$ to $r$ and the \defin{$T$-depth} of $x$, denoted \defin{$\depth_T(x)$}, is the number of edges in $P_T(x)$.
  The maximum $T$-depth of a vertex in $T$ is the \defin{height} of $T$, denoted \defin{$\height(T)$}.
  For each $y\in V(T)$ and each $x\in V(P_T(x))$ (including $y$),  the vertex $x$ is a $T$-ancestor of $y$ and $y$ is a \defin{$T$-descendant} of $x$.  For each $x\in V(T)$, we define \defin{$T_x$} to be the subtree of $T$ induced by all $T$-descendants of $x$.
  For each edge $xy$ of $T$ where $x$ is a $T$-ancestor of $y$, we say that $x$ is the \defin{$T$-parent} of $y$ and $y$ is a \defin{$T$-child} of $x$.  For any set $S\subseteq V(T)$, the \defin{lowest common $T$-ancestor} of $S$ is the node in $\bigcap_{x\in S} V(P_T(x))$ of maximum $T$-depth.
  For each $i\in\N_1$, the \defin{$i$-th layer} of $T$ is $\mathdefin{L_i(T)}:=\{x\in V(T):\depth_T(x)=i-1\}$.
  Thus, the root of $T$ is the only vertex in $L_1$.



A \defin{tree decomposition} of a graph $G$ is a pair $(T,(B_x \mid x \in V(T)))$,
where $T$ is a tree and $B_x \subseteq V(G)$ for every $x \in V(T)$, with the
following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item for every vertex $u$ in $G$, the subgraph of $T$ induced by $\{x \in V(T) \mid u \in B_x\}$ is non-empty and connected; and
	\item for every edge $uv$ in $G$, there exists $x\in V(T)$ such that $u,v\in B_x$.
\end{enumerate}


We call the sets $B_x$ the \defin{bags} of the tree decomposition and the sets $B_x\cap B_y$ for all distinct $x,y\in V(T)$, the \defin{adhesions} of the tree decomposition.
The \defin{width} of a tree decomposition is the maximum size of a bag minus one.
The \defin{adhesion-width} of a tree decomposition is the maximum size of an adhesion.

let $\calT=(T,(B_x \mid x\in V(T)))$ be a tree decomposition of $G$, and let $x$ be a vertex of $T$.

When we use this notation, the tree decomposition will always be obvious from context.}

  \subsection{Weight functions}

  For a set $S$, a \defin{weight function} over $S$ is a mapping $\omega:S\to\R^+$ of the elements of $S$ onto positive real numbers.  When $X$ is a subset of $S$, we use the shorthand $\mathdefin{\omega(X)}:=\sum_{x\in S}\omega(x)$.  When $S:=V(G)$ is the vertex set of a graph $G$ and $H$ is a subgraph of $G$, we use the shorthand $\mathdefin{\omega(H)}:=\sum_{x\in V(H)}\omega(x)$.

  Non-redundant rooted tree decompositions define a useful weight function over the nodes of their indexing trees.
  Let $G$ be a graph and let $\mathcal{T}=(T,(B_x \mid x\in V(T)))$ be a non-redundant rooted tree decomposition.
  Let $r$ be the root of $T$.
  We define $\mathdefin{\omega_\mathcal{T}(r)}:=|B_r|$ and, for each non-root vertex $y$ of $T$, we define   $\mathdefin{\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(y)} = |B_y\setminus B_x|$ where $x$ is the $T$-parent of $y$.  This defines a weight function $\omega_\mathcal{T}:V(T)\to \N$ with total weight $\sum_{x\in V(T)}\omega_\mathcal{T}(x)=|V(G)|$.



  \subsection{Labelling schemes for graphs of bounded degeneracy}

  A graph $G$ is \defin{$d$-degenerate} if every non-empty subgraph of $G$ contains a vertex of degree at most $d$. A graph class $\mathcal{G}$ is $d$-degenerate if every graph in $\mathcal{G}$ is $d$-degenerate. We make use of the following result, which was first used by \citet{muller:local} to find $\lceil6\log n\rceil$-bit adjacency labelling schemes for planar graphs:

  \begin{thm}[\cite{muller:local}]

    For each $d\in\N$, there exists a $\lceil(d+1)\log n\rceil$-bit adjacency labelling scheme for the class of $d$-degenerate graphs.
  \end{thm}

  \subsection{Shannon and Shannon--Fano--Elias Codes}

  A \defin{code} for a set $S$ is an injective function $\lambda:S\to\{0,1\}^*$ that maps each element in $S$ to a distinct binary string.  A code is \defin{prefix-free} if $\lambda(x)$ is not a prefix of $\lambda(y)$ for all distinct $x,y\in S$.
  We make use of the following fundamental result by Shannon.

  \begin{lem}[\citep{shannon:mathematical}]\label{shannon_code}
    Let $S$ be a non-empty set and let $\omega:S\to\R^+$.  Then there exists a prefix-free code $\pi:S\to\{0,1\}^*$ such that $|\pi(x)|\le \log\omega(S)-\log\omega(x)+1$ for each $x\in S$.
  \end{lem}

  \pat{Make a note about being lazy when we describe multi-part labels.  If we assign a label of length $\ell\in O(\log n)$ that is the concatenation of $p$ bitstrings, then we can prefix the label with the Elias coding of $p$ integers that encode the length of each part. The resulting label then has length $\ell+O(p\log\log n)$.}

  \begin{lem}\label{disjoint_union}
    Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a class of graphs that admits a $(\log n+q(n))$-bit adjacency labelling scheme and let $\mathcal{G}'$ be the class of graphs that can be formed by taking the union of a finite number of pairwise vertex-disjoint graphs in $\mathcal{G}$.  Then $\mathcal{G}'$ admits a $(\log n + q(n)+O(\log\log n))$-bit adjacency labelling scheme.
  \end{lem}

  \begin{proof}
     Let $G$ be a graph in $\mathcal{G}'$, so $G:=\bigcup_{i=1}^m G_i$, where $G_1,\ldots,G_m$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint members of $\mathcal{G}$.  Let $\omega:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\N$ be the weight function defined by $\omega(i)=|V(G_i)|$.  Apply \cref{shannon_code} with $S:=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and weight function $\omega$ to obtain a prefix-free code $\pi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\{0,1\}^*$.  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and each $v\in V(G_i)$, define $\pi(v):=\pi(i)$.

     For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, let $\ell_i:V(G_i)\to\{0,1\}^*$ be an adjacency labelling of $G_i$ with at most $\log |V(G_i)|+q(|V(G_i)|)\le \log\omega(i)+q(n)$ bits per label. For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ each $v\in V(G_i)$, we give $v$ the two-part label $\ell(v) := (\pi(v),\ell_i(v))$.  Then $|\ell(v)|\le \log n + q(n)+ O(\log\log n)$.

     Given $\ell(v)$ and $\ell(w)$ for two vertices $v$ and $w$ of $G$, the adjacency tester first checks if $\pi(v)=\pi(w)$. If not, then $v\in V(G_i)$ and $w\in V(G_j)$ for some $i\neq j$, so $vw\not\in E(G)$.  Otherwise, $v,w\in v(G_i)$ for some $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and the tester uses $\ell_i(v)$ and $\ell_i(w)$ to determine if $vw\in E(G_i)$.
  \end{proof}

  \subsection{Separators with Low Pathwidth and Low Path-Adhesion-Width}

  % In this section we develop one of the main tools used in our proof of \cref{main_result}, but first we explain why this tool is needed.
  %
  % It is well-known that a balanced separator of $G$ can be obtained from one bag of a tree-decomposition \cite{robertson.seymour:graph_minors_ii}. That is, for every $n$-vertex graph $G$ and every tree-decomposition $\mathcal{T}:=(B_x:x\in V(T))$ of $G$, there exists $x\in V(T)$ such that each component of $G-B_x$ has at most $n/2$ vertices.  This result and its generalizations are incredibly useful for divide-and-conquer strategies that apply recursion/induction on substantially smaller subproblems defined by some partition of the components of $G-B_x$.  For example, the fact that each component of $G-B_x$ has at most $n/2$ vertices implies that these components can be partitioned among two subgraphs $G_1$ and $G_2$ each having at most $2n/3$ vertices.

Allowing more bags does not help:  For any $X\subseteq V(T)$, $G-\bigcup_{x\in X} B_x$ leaves us with one subproblem containing $n-|X|-1$ vertices.

In addition to this, we need a parameter $\alpha$ that trades off the adhesion-width of of the path-decomposition against the maximum size of any subproblem.    We start with a simple low path-adhesion-width separator that does not fully satisfy our requirements:
  %
  %
  % \pat{Say something about the similar objects used in \url{https://www.mimuw.edu.pl/~bojan/upload/conflicsBojanczykP16.pdf} and \url{https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05195v1}}


  \begin{lem}\label{low_pathwidth_separator}
    Let $T$ be a rooted tree, let $\omega:V(T)\to\R^+$, let $\alpha\in(0,1)$, and let $X_0:=\{x\in V(T):\omega(T_x)>\alpha\cdot\omega(T)\}$.  Then:
    \begin{enumerate}[nosep,nolistsep,label=(\alph*)]
      \item\label{connected} $T[X_0]$ is connected and contains the root of $T$;
      \item\label{skinny} $|X_0\cap L_i(T)|< 1/\alpha$, for each $i\in\N$; and
      \item\label{light} $\omega(C)\le \alpha\cdot\omega(T)$ for each component $C$ of $T-X_0$.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{lem}

  \begin{proof}
    \noindent\ref{connected}:  Let $r$ be the root of $T$.  Since $\alpha <1$, $\omega(T_r)=\omega(T)>\alpha\cdot\omega(T)$, so $X_0$ contains the root $r$.  For each $y\in X_0\setminus\{r\}$, $X_0$ contains the $T$-parent, $x$, of $y$ since $\omega(T_x)\ge \omega(x)+\omega(T_y)>\omega(T_y)>\alpha\cdot\omega(T)$.  Therefore $T[X]$ contains the root $r$ of $T$ and, for each $y\in X\setminus\{r\}$, $X$ contains the $T$-parent of $y$. Thus $T[X]$ is connected.

    \noindent\ref{skinny}:
    Let $X_i:=X\cap L_i(T)$ for each $i\in\N$.  For each $i\in\N$, the subtrees in $\{T_x:x\in X_i\}$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint, so $\omega(T) \ge \sum_{x\in X_i} \omega(T_x)> |X_i|\cdot\alpha\cdot\omega(T)$.  Rewriting this inequality gives $|X_i|<1/\alpha$ for each $i\in\N$.

    \noindent\ref{light}:
    Each component $C$ of $T-X$ is a subtree of $T$ that is rooted at some node $y$ whose $T$-parent is in $X$.  Since  $y\not\in X$, $\omega(C)=\omega(T_y)\le \alpha\cdot\omega(T)$.
  \end{proof}


  A rooted tree $T$ is \defin{$b$-skinny} if $|L_i(T)|\le b$ for each $i\in\N$.
  A rooted tree-decomposition $\mathcal{T}:=(B_x:x\in V(T))$ of a graph $G$ is \defin{$b$-skinny} if $T$ is $b$-skinny. By repeatedly applying \cref{low_pathwidth_separator} with $b=1/\alpha$, we obtain the following result:

  \begin{cor}\label{b_skinny_decomp}
    For every $b>1$, every $n$-vertex graph $G$ and every tree-decomposition $\mathcal{T}:=(T,(B_x:x\in V(T)))$ of $G$, there exists a tree decomposition $\mathcal{T'}:=(T',(B'_x,x\in V(T')))$ of $G$ such that
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item The adhesion-width of $\mathcal{T}'$ is less than or equal to the adhesion-width of $\mathcal{T}$
      \item For each $x\in V(T')$, $\torso{G}{B'_x}$ has a $b$-skinny tree-decomposition $\mathcal{T}_x:=(T^x, (B_y:y\in V(T^x)))$ where $T^x$ is a $b$-skinny subtree of $T$.
      \item For each $x\in V(T')$, $\omega_\mathcal{T'}(T'_x)\le n/b^{\depth_{T'}(x)}$.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{cor}



  \subsection{Weighted Adjacency and Clique-Encoding Labelling Schemes}

  To prove \cref{MainTechnicalStatement}, we need a generalization of the adjacency labelling scheme of \citet{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} for graphs with product structure that supports weights and also assigns labels to cliques.  This motivates the following definitions.

  Let $G$ be a graph and let $\mathcal{K}$ be a subset of the cliques in $G$.
  A \defin{mixed labelling} of $(G,\mathcal{K})$ is a function $\mu:V(G)\cup \mathcal{K}\to\{0,1\}^*$.\footnote{Note that a clique labelling $\lambda$ of $G$ distinguishes between vertices of $G$ and $1$-vertex cliques in $G$; $\lambda(v)$ is not necessarily equal to $\lambda(G[\{v\}])$.} A graph family $\mathcal{G}$ admits a \defin{weighted $(g_1(n),g_2(n))$-waste mixed labelling scheme} if there exists two functions:
  \begin{enumerate}
      \item $A:(\{0,1\}^*)^2\to\{0,1\}$ (the \defin{adjacency tester}); and
      \item $M:(\{0,1\}^*)^3\to\{0,1\}$ (the \defin{membership tester})
  \end{enumerate}
  such that, for each $n\in\N$, each $n$-vertex graph $G^+$ in $\mathcal{G}$, each set $\mathcal{K}$ of cliques in $G^+$, each subgraph $G$ of $G^+$, and each weight function $\omega:V(G^+)\to\N^+$, there exists a mixed labelling $\mu$ of $G^+$ such that
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item for each $v,w\in V(G)$, $A(\lambda(v),\lambda(w))=1$ if and only if $vw\in E(G)$;
    % \item for each $v\in V(G)$, $|\lambda(v)|\le \log \omega(V(G))-\log \omega(v) + g(n)$, where $\Omega=\sum_{x\in V(G)} \omega(x)$ is the total weight of $w$.
    \item for each $v\in V(G^+)$, $|\mu(v)|\le \log \omega(G) - \log\omega(v) + g_1(n)$
    \item\label{clique_id} for each clique $K$ in $\mathcal{K}$ and each vertex $v$ of $K$, there exists a binary string $\kappa(K,v)$ of length at most $g_2(n)$ such that, for each vertex $v'\in V(G^+)$, $M(\mu(K),\mu(v'),\kappa(K,v))=1$ if and only if $v=v'$; and
    \item for each clique $K$ in $\mathcal{K}$, $|\mu(K)|\le \log \omega(G) - \log(\min_{v\in V(K)}(\omega(v))) + g_1(n)$.
  \end{enumerate}
  It is worth pointing out that the string $\kappa(K,v)$ from point~\ref{clique_id} is not necessarily part of any label of any vertex or clique in $G^+$.  Point~\ref{clique_id} only requires that such a string exists for each clique $K$ and each vertex $v$ of $G^+$.

  For weighted mixed labelling schemes we have this analogue of \cref{disjoint_union} for dealing with vertex disjoint unions of graphs:

  \end{lem}

Apply \cref{shannon_code} with $S:=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and weight function $\omega$ to obtain a prefix-free code $\pi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\{0,1\}^*$ where $|\pi_i(v)|\le\log \omega(G^+)-\log \omega(G^+_i)+1$, for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$.     For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, each $v\in V(G_i^+)$, and each $K\in\mathcal{K}_i$, there exists a string $\kappa_i(K,v)$ of length at most $g_2(n)$ such that, for each $v'\in V(G^+_i)$,  $M(\mu_i(K),\mu_i(v'),\kappa_i(K,v))=1$ if and only if $v'=v$.

  \begin{lem}\label{small_height}
     Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a class of graphs that admits a weighted $(g_1(n),g_2(n))$-waste mixed labelling scheme and let $\mathcal{G}'$ be the class of graphs that contains, for each $n\in\N$, every $n$-vertex graph that has a tree-decomposition $\mathcal{T}:=(T,(B_x,x\in V(T))$ of adhesion-width at most $k(n)$, where $\height(T)\le h(n)$ and each torso of $\mathcal{T}$ belongs to $\mathcal{G}$.  Then $\mathcal{G}'$ admits an $f(n)$-bit labelling scheme, where
     \[
       f(n) = \log n + h(n)\cdot g_1(n) + k(n)\cdot g_2(n) + O((h(n)+k(n))\log\log n)
     \]
  \end{lem}

  \begin{proof}
    Let $G$ be a graph in $\mathcal{G}'$, so $G$ has a tree-decomposition $\mathcal{T}:=(T,(B_x,x\in V(T))$ of adhesion-width $k\le k(n)$, where $h:=\height(T)\le h(n)$ and each torso of $\mathcal{T}$ belongs to $\mathcal{G}$.  Let $r$ be the root of $T$, let $A_r:=\emptyset$ and, for each $y\in V(T)\setminus\{r\}$, let $A_y:=B_y\cap B_x$, where $x$ is the $T$-parent of $y$.

    The proof is by induction on $h$, but we require a slightly more detailed inductive hypothesis:  We will produce a labelling $\ell:V(G)\to\{0,1\}^*$ such that, for each $y\in V(T)$ and each $w\in B_y\setminus A_y$,
    \begin{align}
       |\ell(w)| & \le \log n \notag \\
       & \quad {} + |A_y|\cdot (g_2(n) + O(\log\log n))  \label{strong_strong} \\
       & \quad {} + h\cdot(g_1(n) + O(\log\log n)) \notag
    \end{align}

    For each clique $K$ in $\torso{G}{B_r}$, let $Y_K$ be the set of $T$-children $y$ of $r$ such that $B_r\cap B_y=V(K)$. Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the set of cliques $K$ in $\torso{G}{B_r}$ such that $Y_K$ is non-empty. For each clique $K\in\mathcal{K}$ define $\omega(K):=\sum_{y\in Y_K}\omega_\mathcal{T}(T_y)$.  Observe that $\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\omega(K)=\sum_{y\in L_2(T)}\omega_\mathcal{T}(T_y)=n-|B_r|$.

    For each $v\in B_r$, let $\mathcal{K}[v]:=\{K\in\mathcal{K}:v\in V(K)\}$ and let $\omega:V(B_r)\to\N^+$ be the weight function defined by $\omega(v):=k+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}[v]}\omega(K)$.  Since $\mathcal{T}$ has adhesion-width at most $k$, each clique in $\mathcal{K}$ has at most $k$ vertices, so
    \[
       \sum_{v\in B_r}\omega(v)
       = k|B_r| + k\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\omega(K) = (k+1)n \enspace .
    \]
    Let $\mu_r:B_r\to\{0,1\}^*$ be a weighted $(g_1(n),g_2(n))$-waste mixed labelling of $\torso{G}{B_r}$ and $G[B_r]$ with using weight function $\omega$.  Then, for each $v\in B_r$, $|\mu(v)|\le \log \omega(B_r)-\log\omega(v)+g(n)\le \log n - \log \omega(v) + g_1(n)+O(\log k+\log\log n)$.  For $v\in B_r$, $\mu(v)$ is the entire label of $v$:
    \[  \ell(v) := \mu(v) \qquad\text{for each $v\in B_r$.} \]

    For each $z\in V(T)$, let $C_z:=B_z\setminus B_r$.
    Let $K\in \mathcal{K}$ and $y\in Y_K$.  Define $V_y:=\bigcup_{z\in V(T_y)} B_z\setminus B_r$, and $G_y:=G[V_y]$.  Then $\mathcal{T}_y:=\{T_y, C_z:z\in V(T_y)\}$ is a tree-decomposition of $G_y$ and and $\height(T_y)\le h-1$. Apply induction on $G_y$ to get an adjacency labelling $\ell_y:B_y\to\{0,1\}^*$.  For each $z\in V(T_y)$ and each $w\in C_z\setminus A_z$,
    \begin{align*}
       |\ell_y(w)| & \le \log |V_y| \\
       & \quad {} + h\cdot(g_1(n) + O(\log\log n)) \\
       & \quad {} + |A_z\setminus B_r|\cdot (g_2(n) + O(\log\log n))
    \end{align*}
    For each $K\in\mathcal{K}$, let $V_K:=\bigcup_{y\in Y_K} V_y$ and let $G_K:=G[V_K]=\bigcup_{y\in Y_K} G_y$.  By \cref{disjoint_union}, there exists an adjacency labelling $\ell_K:V_K\to\{0,1\}^*$ such that, for each $y\in Y_K$, each $z\in V(T_y)$ and each $w\in C_z\setminus A_z$,
    \begin{align*}
       |\ell_K(w)| & \le \log |V_K| \\
       & \quad {} + h\cdot(g_1(n) + O(\log\log n)) \\
       & \quad {} + |A_z\setminus B_r|\cdot (g_2(n) + O(\log\log n))
    \end{align*}
    By the definition of mixed labelling scheme, for each $v\in A_z\cap B_r$, there exists a string $\kappa(v,K)$ of length at most $g_2(n)$ such that, for any $v'\in B_r$, $M(\mu(K),\mu(v'),\kappa(v,K))=1$ if and only if $v'=v$.

    For each $K\in\mathcal{K}$, each $y\in Y_K$, each $z\in V(T_y)$, and each $w\in C_z\setminus A_z$, let
    \[  \alpha(w):=(\kappa(v,K): v\in B_z\cap B_r\cap N_G(w)) \]
     and assign the label
    \[
       \ell(w) := (\mu(K)\,,\, \ell_K(w)\,,\, \alpha(w) )
    \]
    The third part of $\ell(w)$ has length at most $|A_z\cap B_r|\cdot (g_2(n) + O(\log\log n))$, so the length of $\ell(w)$ satisfies \eqref{strong_strong}.

    \paragraph{Adjacency Testing:}
    Given the labels $\ell(v)$ and $\ell(w)$ for two vertices $u$ and $w$ of $G$, the adjacency tester proceeds as follows:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item If $v$ and $w$ are both in $B_r$, then the adjacency tester uses $\mu(v)$ and $\mu(w)$ to test if $vw\in E(G[B_r])$.
      \item If exactly one of $v$ or $w$ in $B_r$ then, assume without loss of generality that $v\in B_r$ and $w\in G_K$ for some $K\in\mathcal{K}$. In this case, the adjacency tester uses $\alpha(w)$, $\mu(K)$, and $\mu(v)$ to test if $v=v'$ for some $v'\in N_G(w)\cap B_r$.
      \item Otherwise, $v\in G_K$ and $w\in G_{K'}$ for some $K,K'\in\mathcal{K}$.  If $\mu(K)\neq\mu(K')$ then $K\neq K'$, and $V(K)$ separates $v$ and $w$, so $vw\not\in E(G)$. If $K=K'$, then the adjacency tester uses $\ell_K(v)$ and $\ell_K(w)$ to test if $vw\in E(G_k)$.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{proof}


  \begin{lem}\label{skinny_labelling}
    Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a graph class that admits a weighted $(g_1(n),g_2(n))$-waste mixed labelling scheme and let $\mathcal{G}'$ be the class of graphs consisting of every graph that has a $b$-skinny tree-decomposition of adhesion-width at most $k$ in which each torso is a member of $\mathcal{G}$.  Then $\mathcal{G}'$ admits a weighted $(g_1'(n),g_2'(n))$-waste mixed labelling scheme, where
    \[ g'_1(n) = g_1(n) + O(k(\log(kb) + \log\log n)) \]
    \[ g'_2(n) = \max\{g_2(n)+O(1), O(\log(kb)+\log\log n)\} \]
  \end{lem}

  \begin{proof}
    Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex graph in $\mathcal{G}'$, let $G$ be a spanning subgraph of $G^+$, let $\mathcal{K}$ be a set of cliques in $G^+$, and let $\omega:V(G^+)\to\N^+$ be a weight function.
    By definition, $G^+$ has a $b$-skinny tree decomposition $\mathcal{T}:=(T,(B_x:x\in V(T)))$ in which each torso belongs to $\mathcal{G}$.  For each $i\in\N$, let $B_i:=\bigcup_{x\in L_i(T)} B_x$.  Let $p:=\max\{j\in\N: B_j\neq\emptyset\}$.  Then $B_1,\ldots,B_p$ is a path decomposition of $G$ of adhesion-width at most $kb$.
    % By definition, $G^+$ has a path decomposition $B_1,\ldots,B_p$ in which each torso belongs to $\mathcal{G}$.
    Let $A_1:=\emptyset$ and, for each $i\in\{2,\ldots,p\}$, let $A_i:=B_i\cap B_{i-1}$.

    \paragraph{\boldmath The layer label $\rho(v)$:}
    Define the weight function $\omega:\{1,\ldots,p\}\to\N^+$ by $\omega(i):=\omega(B_i\setminus A_i)$.  Since $\{B_i\setminus A_i:i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}$ is a partition of $V(G)$,  $\sum_{i=1}^p\omega(i)=\omega(G^+)$. Apply \cref{shannon_fano_elias_tree} and \cref{shannon_fano_elias_code} to the set $S:=\{1,\ldots,p\}$ with weight function $\omega$, and let $T_\omega$ and $\rho:S\to\{0,1\}^*$ be the resulting binary tree and code, respectively.  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$ and each $v\in B_i\setminus A_i$, define $\rho(v):=\rho(i)$.

    \paragraph{\boldmath The mixed label $\mu'(v)$:}
    For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$, let $G_i^+:=\bigcup_{x\in L_i(T)}\torso{G}{B_x}-A_i$, let $G_i:=\bigcup_{x\in L_i}G[B_x]-A_i$, and let $\mathcal{K}_i:=\{K[V(K)\cap B_i\setminus A_i]-:K\in\mathcal{K}\}\setminus\{\emptyset\}$.\footnote{Here, $\emptyset$ is used to denote the graph (clique) with no vertices, which we do not want to appear in $\mathcal{K}_i$.}  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$, apply \cref{disjoint_union_ii} to $G_i^+$, $G_i$, and $\mathcal{K}_i$ with weight function $\omega_i$ to obtain a mixed labelling $\mu_i:V(G^+_i)\cup\mathcal{K}\to\{0,1\}^*$, which includes a string $\kappa_i(K,v)$ for each $K\in\mathcal{K}_i$ and $v\in V(K)$. For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$ and each $v\in B_i\setminus A_i$,  let $\mu'(v):=\mu_i(v)$.

    %
    % For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$, each $K\in\mathcal{K}_i$, each $v\in V(K)$, let $\rho(K):=\rho(i)$, let $\mu'(K):=\mu_i(K)$, and let $\kappa'(K,v)=\kappa_i(K,v)$.
    % As the proof of \cref{disjoint_union_ii} shows, labelling each vertex $v$ of $G^+$ with $(rho(v),\mu'(v))$ and each clique $K\in\bigcup_{i=1}^p\mathcal{K}_i$ with $(\rho(K),\mu'(K))$ is sufficient for adjacency testing in $G_0:=\bigcup_{i=1}^p(G_i)$ and membership testing for any clique $K\in \bigcup_{i=1}^p\mathcal{K}_i$.  What remains is augment this labelling in order to deal with edges $vw$ of $G$ with $w\in B_i\setminus A_i$ and $v\in A_i$ and for cliques $K\in\mathcal{K}$ with $V(K)\cap A_i\neq\emptyset$, for  some $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$.

    \paragraph{\boldmath The adhesion label $\kappa(v)=(d(v),\varphi(v))$:}
    Let $A:=\bigcup_{i=1}^p A_i$.  For each vertex $v\in A$, let $a(v):=\min\{i:v\in B_i\}$ and $b(v):=\max\{i:v\in B_i\}$.  Note that, for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$ and each $v\in A_i$, $a(v)\le i-1$ and $b(v)\ge i$, so $a(v)$ and $b(v)$ are two different leaves of $T_\omega$, so $\lca_{T_\omega}(a(v),b(v))$ is a node of $T_\omega$ with two children.  Let $x_{T_\omega}:A\to V(T_\omega)$ be the function defined by  $x_{T_\omega}(v):=\lca_{T_\omega}(a(v),b(v))$.  For each $x\in V(T)$, let $x_{T_\omega}^{-1}(x):=\{v\in A:x_{T_\omega}(v)=x\}$ be the preimage of $x$ in $x_{T_\omega}$.

    We now argue that, for each $x\in V(T)$, $|x_{T_\omega}^{-1}(x)|\le kb$. If $x$ is a node of $T_\omega$ with less than two children then $x_{T_\omega}^{-1}(x)=\emptyset$, so assume that $x$ has at least two children.  Then the smallest leaf $T_\omega$-descendant of the right child of $x$ is $i$ and the largest leaf $T_\omega$-descendant of the left child of $x$ is $i-1$, for some $i\in\{2,\ldots,p\}$.  For each $v\in A$, $v\in B_{a(v)}\cap\cdots\cap B_{b(v)}$. For each $v\in x_{T_\omega}^{-1}(x)$,   $a(v)\le i-1$ and $b(v)\ge i$, so $v\in B_{i-1}$ and $v\in B_i$.  Therefore $|x_{T_\omega}^{-1}(x)|\le |B_{i-1}\cap B_i|\le bk$, since $B_1,\ldots,B_p$ has adhesion-width at most $bk$.  Therefore, each $v\in A$ can be assigned a colour $\varphi(v)\in\{1,\ldots,kb\}$ that is different from $\varphi(v')$ for each $v'\in x_{T_\omega}^{-1}(x_{\omega}(v))$.

    Observe that, for any $v,v'\in A$,  $v=v'$ if and only if $(x_{T_\omega}(v),\varphi(v)) = (x_{T_\omega}(v'),\varphi(v'))$.
    For each $v\in A$, let $d(v):=\depth_{T_\omega}(x_{T_\omega}(v))$.
    Recall that, for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$,  $\rho(i)$ encodes the root-to-$i$ in $T_\omega$ as a sequence of zeros and ones.  In particular, for each $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,|\rho(i)|\}$, the first $\ell$ bits of $\rho(i)$ encode the path from the root of $T_\omega$ to the depth-$\ell$ $T_\omega$-ancestor of $i$. This has the following useful consequences:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
      \item For any $v,v'\in A$, $v=v'$ if and only if $d(v)=d(v')$,  $\rho(v)[1,\ldots,d(v)]=\rho(v')[1,\ldots,d(v)]$, and $\varphi(v)=\varphi(v')$.

      \item For any $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$, any $w\in B_i\setminus A_i$ and any $v\in A$,  $v\in A_i$ if and only $\rho(v)[1,\ldots,d(v)]=\rho(w)[1,\ldots,d(v)]$.
    \end{enumerate}
    These observations motivate the following definitions:
    For each $v\in A$, let $\kappa(v)$ be a binary string of length $\log(kb)+O(\log\log n)$ that encodes $\varphi(v)$ and $d(v)$.
    For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$ and each $w\in B_i\setminus A_i$, let $\alpha(w):=(\kappa(v):v\in N_G(w)\cap A_i)$.


    \paragraph{\boldmath The vertex label $\mu(v)$:}
    For each $v\in V(G^+)$, the label for $v$ is
    \[
      \mu(v):=
      \begin{cases}
        (\rho(v)\,,\, \mu(v)\,,\, \alpha(v)\,,\, \kappa(v))
        & \text{if $v\in A$} \\
        (\rho(v)\,,\, \mu(v)\,,\, \alpha(v))
        & \text{if $v\not\in A$.}
      \end{cases}
    \]
    We call $\kappa(v)$ the \defin{optional part} of $\mu(v)$.
    With this definition,
    \[  |\mu(v)|\le \omega(G^+)-\omega(v) + g_1(n) + O(k(\log(kb)+\log\log n)) \enspace ,
    \]
    where the first three terms come from $\rho(v)=\rho(i)$ and $\mu(v)=\mu_i(v)$ and the final two terms accounts for the at most $k$ parts in $\alpha(v)$ and the optional part $\kappa(v)$. This describes the labelling of $V(G^+)$.

    \paragraph{\boldmath The clique labels $\mu(K)$ and membership labels $\kappa(K,v)$:}
    Fix some $K\in\mathcal{K}$ and let $i:=\min\{\iota:V(K)\cap B_\iota\neq\emptyset\}$. By the minimality of $i$, $V(K)\cap B_i\setminus A_i=V(K)\cap B_i$. Let $j:=\max\{\iota:V(K)\cap B_\iota\setminus A_\iota\neq\emptyset\}$.  Then $j$ is well-defined and $i\le j$, since $V(K)\cap B_i\setminus A_i\neq\emptyset$. By the choice of $j$, $K-A_j$ is non-empty, so $K-A_j\in\mathcal{K}_j$. Therefore $K-A_j$ is assigned a label $\mu_j(K-A_j)$ in the labelling of $G^+_j$.  We define the clique label
    \[
        \mu(K):=(\rho(K),\mu_j(K)) \enspace .
    \]
    Each vertex $v$ of $K-A_j$ is assigned a string $\kappa_j(K-A_j,v)$.
    For each $v\in V(K)$, we define
    \[
         \kappa(K,v):=
         \begin{cases}
           (0,\kappa_j(K-A_j,v)) & \text{$v\in B_j\setminus A_j$} \\
           (1,\kappa(v)) & \text{if $v\in A_j$.}
         \end{cases}
     \]
     In the first case, $|\kappa(K,v)|\le g_2(n)+O(1)$. In the second case $|\kappa(K,v)|\in O(\log(kb)+\log\log n)$.

     \paragraph{Adjacency Testing:}
     Given the labels $\mu(v)$ and $\mu(w)$ for two vertices $v$ and $w$ in $G$, the adjacency tester first compares $\rho(v)$ and $\rho(w)$:
     \begin{itemize}
       \item If $\rho(v)=\rho(w)$ then $v$ and $w$ are both vertices of $G_i$, for some $i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$.  In this case the tester uses $\mu'(v)=\mu_i(v)$ and $\mu'(w)=\mu_i(w)$ to test if $vw\in E(G_i)$.
       \item If $\rho(v)\neq\rho(w)$ then assume without loss of generality that $\rho(v)$ is lexicographically less than $\rho(w)$.  In this case $v\in B_i\setminus A_i$ and $w\in B_j\setminus A_j$ for some $i < j$.  The edge $vw$ can only be present in $G$ if $v\in A_j$.  First the tester checks the optional part of $\mu(v)$ to see if $v\in A$. If not, then the tester outputs $0$.  If so, then the tester uses $d(v)$, $\rho(v)$, and $\rho(w)$ to check if $v\in A_i$. If not, then the tester outputs $0$.  If so, then the tester checks if $\kappa(v)$ is in $\alpha(w)$. If not, then the tester outputs $0$. If so, then the tester outputs $1$.
     \end{itemize}

     \paragraph{Memberhip Testing:}
     Given the labels $\mu(K)$, $\mu(v')$, and $\kappa(K,v)$ for some $K\in\mathcal{K}$, some $v'\in V(G^+)$, and some $v\in V(K)$, the membership tester first compares $\rho(K)$ and $\rho(v')$.
     \begin{itemize}
       \item If $\rho(K)$ is lexicograpically smaller than $\rho(v')$, then $V(K)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^j B_i$ and $v'\in B_{j'}\setminus A_{j'}$ for some $j < j'$. The definition of $\rho(K)$ implies that $V(K)\cap B_{j'}\setminus A_{j'}=\emptyset$, so $v'\neq v$, since $v\in V(K)$.


       \item If $\rho(K)=\rho(v')$ then $K-A_j\in\mathcal{K}_j$, and $v'\in B_j\setminus A_j$ for some $j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$. In this case, $v$ can only be equal to $v'$ if $v\in B_j\setminus A_j$.  The membership tester checks this condition by looking at the first bit of $\kappa(K,v)$. If $v\in B_j\setminus A_j$ then the membership tester uses $\mu_j(K)$ (contained in $\mu(K)$), $\mu'(v')=\mu_j(v')$ (contained in $\mu(v)$), and   $\kappa_j(K,v)$ (contained in $\kappa(K,v)$) to test if $v'=v$.

       \item If $\rho(K)$ is lexicograpically larger than $\rho(v')$ then $K-A_j\in\mathcal{K}_j$ and $v'\in B_i\setminus A_i$ for some $i < j$.  In this case, $v'$ can only be equal to $v$ if $v'\in A_j$.  The membership tester first uses the optional part of $\mu(v')$ to check if $v'\in A$. If not, then it outputs $0$.  If so, then it uses $d(v')$, $\rho(v')=\rho(i)$ and $\rho(K)=\rho(j)$ to check if $v'\in A_j$. If not, then it outputs $0$.  If so, then it uses the first bit of $\kappa(K,v)$ to check if $v\in A_j$. If not, it outputs $0$. If so, it checks if $\kappa(v)$ (contained in $\kappa(K,v)$) is equal to $\kappa(v')$ (contained in the optional part of $\mu(v')$.)
     \end{itemize}

  \end{proof}

  \begin{rem}
    The proof of \cref{skinny_labelling} extends to the slightly more general setting in which the class $\mathcal{G}'$ consists of graphs $G$ that have a path decomposition $B_1,\ldots,B_p$ of adhesion-width at most $bk$ whose torsos are in $\mathcal{G}$ and for which $|N_G(w)\cap B_{i-1}|\le k$ for each $i\in\{2,\ldots,p\}$ and each $w\in B_i\setminus B_{i-1}$.
  \end{rem}


  \begin{lem}\label{RealMainTechnical}
    Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a graph class that admits a weighted $(g_1(n),g_2(n))$-waste mixed labelling scheme and let $\mathcal{G}'$ be the class of graphs consisting of every graph that has a tree-decomposition of adhesion-width at most $k$ in which each torso is a member of $\mathcal{G}$.  Then $\mathcal{G}'$ admits an $f(n)$-bit adjacency labelling scheme, where
    \[ f(n) = \log n + O\left(\sqrt{k\cdot g_1(n)\cdot\log n} + k\cdot g_2(n) + {??}\right) \]
  \end{lem}

  \begin{proof}
    Let $b=2^{\sqrt{k\cdot g_1(n)\cdot\log n}}$. Apply \cref{b_skinny_decomp} to get a tree decomposition of heigth $h:=\log_b n$ where each torso has a $b$-skinny tree-decomposition. By \cref{skinny_labelling}, each torso has a weighted mixed labelling.  By \cref{small_height}, the whole graph has an adjacency labelling.
  \end{proof}

  In this appendix, we review the labelling scheme of \citet{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} and its improvement by \citet{alstrup.rauhe:improved} in order to show the existence of the weighted mixed labellings promised in \cref{gk_labelling}.
  Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex subgraph of $H\boxtimes P$, where $H$ is a graph of treewidth $k$ and $P$ is a path.  Without loss of generality, we assume that the vertices of $P$ are the integers $1,\ldots,h$, in order.   Also without loss of generality, we assume that $H$ is an edge-maximal graph of treewidth $k$.  This implies that $H$ has an acyclic orientation in which the vertices in the out-neighbourhood $N^+_H(v)$ form a clique in $H$, for each $v\in V(H)$.


  \paragraph{\boldmath Mapping $V(H)$ onto intervals:}
  The first step in \cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} is an idea used by \citet{gavoille.labourel:shorter} in their labelling scheme for bounded-treewidth graphs.  Each vertex $v$ of $H$ is mapped to a real interval \defin{$f(v)$}. This mapping has the following properties (for some fixed $c>0$):
  \begin{enumerate}
      \item\label{nested_or_disjoint} For any two vertices $v,w\in V(H)$, $f(v)\subseteq f(w)$, $f(w)\subseteq f(v)$ or $f(v)\cap f(w)=\emptyset$.
      \item\label{intersection_subgraph} For each $vw\in E(H)$, $f(v)\cap f(w)\neq\emptyset$.
      \item\label{thin} For each $x\in R$, $|\{v\in V(H):x\in f(v)\}|\le ck\log n$.
  \end{enumerate}
  \pat{Remove this paragraph?} The existence of such a mapping $f$ is easily established by recursive procedure  that takes an open interval $(a,b)$ and a subgraph $H'$ of $H$.  The procedure identifies a set $S$ of at most $k+1$ vertices  in $H'$ such that the components of $H'-S$ can be partitioned into two graph $H'_1$ and $H'_2$, each with at most $\tfrac{2}{3}|V(H')|$ vertices. For each $v\in S$, $f(v):=(a,b)$.  The procedure is then applied inductively to map the vertices of $H'_1$ onto subintervals of $(a,(a+b)/2)$ and then again to map the vertices of $H'_2$ onto subintervals of $((a+b)/2,b)$.

  Let $I$ be the interval intersection graph with vertex set $V(I):=V(H)$ and edge set $E(I):=\{vw\in\binom{V(H)}{2}:f(v)\cap f(w)\neq\emptyset$.  Property~\ref{intersection_subgraph} implies that $H$ is a subgraph of $I$. Property~\ref{thin} implies that $H$ has no clique larger than $ck\log n$.  The graph $I$ has a proper colouring $\mathdefin{\varphi}:V(H)\to\{1,\ldots,\lfloor ck\log n\rfloor\}$ \cite{gilmore.hoffman:characterization}.  The label for each vertex $(v,i)$ of $G$ will include the $O(\log\log (tn))$-bit integer $\varphi(v)$.

  \paragraph{\boldmath Mapping $V(H)$ onto points:}
  For each vertex $v$ of $H$, let $x_f(v)$ be the midpoint of the interval $f(v)$.\footnote{Any real number $x_f(v)$ in the interior of the interval $f(v)$ would also work.}  Property~\ref{nested_or_disjoint} then implies
  \begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{3}
      \item\label{clique_property} For any clique $K$ in $I$, there exists $w\in V(K)$ such $x_f(w)\in f(v)$ for each $v\in V(K)$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Indeed, a vertex $w$ of $K$ that minimizes the length of $f(w)$ satisfies this condition.

  \paragraph{\boldmath Treating $G$ as a sequence of graphs}

  Each vertex of $G$ is a pair $(v,i)$ with an \defin{$H$-coordinate} $v\in V(H)$ and a \defin{$P$-coordinate} $i\in V(P)$.  We may assume, without loss of generality, that $G$ contains at least one vertex with $P$-coordinate $i$, for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$.

  The next idea in \cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} is to consider a sequence of subgraphs $R_1,\ldots,R_h\subseteq H\boxtimes P$, where $R_i:=(H\boxtimes P)[(v,j)\in V(G):j\in\{i-1,i,i+1\}]$. Each vertex of $G$ appears in at most three of these subgraphs, so $\sum_{i=2}^1 |V(R_i)|\le 3n$.

  The label of any vertex $(v,i)$ of $G$ has two main parts, whose lengths sum to $\log n+o(\log n)$. The first part, $\lambda_1((v,i))$ is determined by the $P$-coordinate, $i$.  The second part, $\lambda_2((v,i))$, is obtained from a labelling of $R_i$, so it represents the $H$-coordinate, $v$.

      % \item\label{plus_increase} $|S^+_i| \le 2|S^+_{i-1}|$ for each $i\in\{2,\ldots,r\}$; and

  We remark that the contents of the sets $S^+_1,\ldots,S^+_h$ are entirely determined by the contents of the sets $S_1,\ldots,S_h$.  (Formally, the sequence $S^+_1,\ldots,S^+_h$ is the result of applying a function $q:(2^\R)^*\to (2^\R)^*$ whose input is a sequence of sets of real numbers and whose output is a sequence of sets of real numbers.   With the sets $S^+_1,\ldots,S^+_h$ described, we can now discuss $\lambda_1$.

This part is obtained by applying \cref{shannon_fano_elias_code} to the set $S:=\{1,\ldots,h\}$ using the weight function $i \mapsto |S^+_i|$ to obtain a prefix-free code $\mathdefin{\rho}:\{1,\ldots,h\}\to\{0,1\}^*$. For each $i\in\{2,\ldots,h\}$, a string $\mathdefin{\mu(i)}$ of length $O(\log\log n)$ is computed so that $\rho(i-1)$ can be derived from $\rho(i)$ and $\mu(i)$.  Then
  \[
     \mathdefin{\lambda_1((v,i))}:=(\rho(i),\mu(i))
  \]
  for each vertex $(v,i)$ of $G$.

  \paragraph{Bulk trees:}

  For each $(w,j)\in X_{v,i}$, we have $x_f(w)\in S_i\subseteq S^+_i=V(T_i)$. Therefore, $x_{T_i}(w)$ and $\sigma((w,i))$ are well-defined, even when $j\neq i$ and $(w,i)\not\in V(G)$.

     \mathdefin{\pi((v,i))}:= \left( \quad(|\sigma((w,i))|,\, \varphi(w),\, j-i,\, b(w,j))\quad :\quad (w,j)\in X_{v,i}\right) \enspace . \label{four_tuple}
  \end{equation}
  where $b(w,j)$ is a single bit that indicates, for each $(w,j)\in X_{v,i}$ whether or not $(v,i)(w,j)\in E(G)$.

(The preceding sentence does not contain a typo! The occurrences of $i$ and $j$ are deliberate.) Given only these values, it is straightforward to test if $(v,i)
  (w,j)$ is an edge of $G$.  Quickly: Using $\lambda_1((v,i))$ and $\lambda_1((w,j))$, the tester checks if $|j-i|\le 1$.  If not, then $(v,i)$ and $(w,j)$ are not adjacent in $G$. The label for $(v,i)$ contains all the information the tester needs about $(v,i)$, but the label for $(w,i-1)$ does not contain $\sigma^+((w,i))$. Instead, it contains $\sigma^+((w,i-1))$.  The extended signature $\sigma^+((w,i-1)$ describes a path $P_{T_{i-1}}(x_{T_{i-1}}(w))$ in $T_{i-1}$, not a path in $T_i$.  To determine if $(v,i)$ and $(w,i-1)$ are adjacent in $R_i$ (and therefore $(v,i)$ and $(w,i-1)$ are potentially adjacent in $G$) the tester needs the extended signature $\sigma^+((w,i))$ that describes $P_{T_i}(x_{T_i}(w))$. \begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{gak_labelling}]
  Let $G^+,G\in\mathcal{G}_{a,k}$, $\mathcal{K}\subseteq K(G^+)$, and $\omega:V(G^+)\cup\mathcal{K}\to\N^+$ be as in the definition of mixed labelling scheme.  Then each of $G^+$ and $G$ is a subgraph of $(H\boxtimes P)\oplus K_a$.  Let $A:=V(K_a)\cap V(G^+)$ be the set of vertices in $G^+$ that map to vertices of $K_a$.  Then $G^+-A$ and $G-A$ are each in $\mathcal{G}_k$.  Apply \cref{gk_labelling}, to $G^+-A$, $G-A$, $\omega$, and $\{K-A:K\in\mathcal{K}\}$ to obtain a mixed labelling $\lambda:V(G^+-A)\to\{0,1\}^*$.  Then $|\lambda(v)|\le \log\omega(G^+-A) -\log\omega(v)+o(\log n)\le \log\omega(G^+)-\log\omega(v)+o(\log n)$ for each $v\in V(G^+-A)$.

  Extend the domain of $\lambda$ to include the vertices in $A$ by setting $\lambda(v):=\varepsilon$ (the empty string), for each $v\in A$.  Let $u_1,\ldots,u_{a'}$ be an ordering of the vertices in $A$. For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,a'\}$, define $\sigma(u_i)$ to be the $\lceil\log(a+1)\rceil$-bit binary representation of $i$.  For each $v\in V(G-A)$, define $\sigma(v)$ to be a sequence of $\lceil\log(a+1)\rceil$ zero bits.  For each $v\in V(G)$ and each $i\in\{1,\ldots,a'\}$ let $b_i(v)$ be a bit that indicates if $vu_i\in E(G)$.  Finally, define $\lambda'(v):=\sigma(v),b_1(v),\ldots,b_{a'}(v),\lambda(v)$ for each $v\in V(G)$.  Then $|\lambda'(v)|=|\lambda(v)|+\lceil\log (a+1)\rceil+a \le \log\omega(G)-\log\omega(v)+o(\log n)$.

  Adjacency testing in $G$ using $\lambda'$ is straightforward. For any two vertices $v$ and $w$, $\sigma(v)$ and $\sigma(w)$ can be used to determine if $v$ and/or $w$ are in $A$.  If at least one of them, say $v$, is in $A$ then $b_{\sigma(v)}(w)$ indicates whether $vw\in E(G)$.  If neither $v$ nor $w$ is in $A$, then the presence of the edge $vw$ can be tested using $\lambda(v)$ and $\lambda(w)$.

  Next we extend $\lambda'(v)$ so that its domain contains the cliques in $\mathcal{K}$.  First, extend the domain of $\lambda$ so that it includes the zero-vertex clique $K_0$, by setting $\lambda(K_0):=\varepsilon$ (the empty string).
  For each $K\in\mathcal{K}$, the labelling $\lambda$ imposes an order $v_1,\ldots,v_{k''}$ on the vertices of $K-A$. Extend this to the vertices of $K$ by appending the vertices of $V(K)\cap A$ in the order that they appear in $u_1,\ldots,u_{a}$. This assigns an ordering $v_1,\ldots,v_{k'}$ to $V(K)$ for each $K\in\mathcal{K}$.
  For each $K\in\mathcal{K}$ and each $j\in\{1,\ldots,a\}$ let $c_i(K)$ be a single bit that indicates if $u_j\in V(K)$.  Let $\alpha(K)=|A\cap V(K)|$ and let $\beta(K)=|V(K-A)|$.  Then, for each $K\in\mathcal{K}$, define $\lambda'(K):=\alpha(K),\beta(K),c_1(K),\ldots,c_{a}(K),\lambda(K-A)$.

  Now, consider some clique $K\in\mathcal{K}$.  If $V(K)\subseteq A$, then
  \[
     |\lambda'(K)|\le O(a + \log k) + |\lambda(K_0)| \le\log\omega(G^+) - \log\min\{\omega(v):v\in V(K)\} + o(\log n) \enspace .
  \]
  Otherwise,
  \begin{align*}
     |\lambda'(K)|
     & \le O(a + \log k) +|\lambda(K-A)| \\
     & \le \log\omega(G^+)-\log\min\{\omega(v):v\in V(K-A)\} + o(\log n) \\
     & \le \log\omega(G^+)-\log\min\{\omega(v):v\in V(K)\} + o(\log n) \enspace .
  \end{align*}

  Let $K\in\mathcal{K}$, $v\in V(G^+)$, and $i\in\{1,\ldots,2k+a\}$.
  Given $\lambda'(K)$, $\lambda'(v)$, and $i$ we can do membership testing as follows:
  Let $v_1,\ldots,v_{k'}$ be the ordering we have assigned to the vertices of $K$.
  \begin{itemize}
      \item If $i> \beta(K)+\alpha(K)$ then $i > k'$ and we can immediately determine that $v\neq v_i$.
      \item If $i \le \beta(K)$ then $v_i$ is a vertex of $G^+-A$.  If $\sigma(v)\neq 0$, then $v\in A$ and we can immediately determine that $v\neq v_i$.  Otherwise, $\lambda(K)$ and $\lambda(v)$ can be used to test if $v=v_i$.
      \item If $\beta(K) < i \le \beta(K)+\alpha(K)$ then $v_i\in A$. In fact, $v_i=u_j$, where $j$ is the smallest integer such that $i = \beta(K) + \sum_{\ell=1}^j c_\ell(K)$.  Thus, we can determine that $v_i=u_j$ and then test if $v=v_i=u_j$ by checking if (the $\lceil\log (a+1)\rceil$-bit binary representation of) $j$ is equal to $\sigma(v)$. \qedhere
  \end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{B-trees:}

  In \cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved}, the authors use weight-balanced B-trees to replace the (binary) bulk trees used in \cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency}.  A \defin{B-tree} stores a set of real numbers in the leaves of a (not usually binary) tree $B$ in which all leaves have the same depth. \defin{Weight-balanced B-trees} are parameterized by a parameter $a\ge 6$.  A weight-balanced B-tree $B$ is \defin{semi-balanced} if, for each node $x$ of $B$, the number of leaves in the subtree $B_x$ is at most $6a^{\height(B_x)}$ and for each \emph{non-root} node $x$ of $B$, the number of leaves in $B_x$ is at least $\tfrac{1}{2}a^{\height(B_x)})$.

  Let $B$ be a weight-balanced B-tree with $r\in \{1,\ldots,n\}$ leaves.  The weight-balance conditions guarantee that $\height(B)\le \log_a r + 1$.  The weight-balance conditions also ensure that the number of children of any node $x$ of $B$ is at most $12a$.  This implies that any root-to-leaf path in a B-tree can be encoded by a sequence of $\log_a r+1$ integers, each requiring $\lceil\log(12a)\rceil\le \log a + 5$ bits to represent, for a total of at most $\lceil\log(12a)\rceil\cdot(\log_a r+1)=\log r + O(\log_a r + \log a)$ bits.  The value of $a$ used in \cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved} is approximately $2^{\sqrt{\log n}}$, so that any root-to-leaf path in $B$ can be represented using $\log r + O(\sqrt{\log n})$ bits.

  The sequence of binary search trees $T_1,\ldots,T_h$ used in \cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved} is replaced with a sequence of B-trees $B_1,\ldots,B_h$.
  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$,  the leaves of $B_i$ store the values in $S^+_i$.  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$, each vertex $(v,j)\in V(R_i)$ is mapped to the lowest-common-$B_i$-ancestor $x_{B_i}(v)$ of $f(v)\cap S^+_i$.  Then, for a vertex $(v,i)$ of $G$, the signature $\sigma((v,i))$ is the encoding of  $P_{B_i}(x_{B_i}(v))$.  Then
  \[
    |\sigma((v,i))| \le \lceil\log (12a)\rceil\cdot(\depth_{B_i}(x_{B_i}(v))) = \lceil\log (12a)\rceil(\depth_{B_i}(\lca_{B_i}(f(v)\cap S_i))) \enspace .
  \]
  An upper bound on $|\sigma((v,i))|$ can be obtained by using the $\log_a |S^+_i|+1$ upper bound on $\height(B_i)$:
  \[
    |\sigma((v,i))|\le \lceil\log (12a)\rceil\height(B_i) \le \lceil\log (12a)\rceil(\log_a |S^+_i| + 1)= \log |S^+_i| + O(\sqrt{\log n})
  \]
  The main advantage of using B-trees over the bulk trees in \cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} is that B-trees the rebalancing operations needed to maintain the height bound result in a much simpler transition code $\tau((w,i-1))$.  This simplicity ultimately comes from the fact that, for any $p\in S_{i-1}\cap S_i$, the root-to-$p$ path $P_{B_{i-1}}(p)$ in $B_{i-1}$ and the root-to-$p$ path $P_{B_i}(p)$ in $B_i$ differ by at most a single vertex.  More precisely, a subpath $xyz$ in $P_{B_{i-1}}(p)$ can be replaced by a subpath $xy'z$ to give $P_{B_i}(x)$.\footnote{There are two easily handled exceptions.  Occasionally, $P_{B_i}(x)$ is obtained by removing the first vertex of $P_{B_{i-1}}(x)$ or by adding a vertex at the beginning of $P_{B_{i-1}}(x)$.  These are easily handled distractions, so we ignore them here.}  In terms of the signature function $\sigma$, this means that the difference between these two paths can be described by an $O(\log\log_a n)$-bit integer that gives the index of the vertex $y$ being replaced and two $\lceil\log (12a)\rceil$ bit integers that describe where $y'$ is in the list of $x$'s children and where $z$ is in the list of $y'$'s children.\footnote{Determining $\sigma^+((w,i))$ from $\sigma^+((w,i-1))$ requires a bit more work, since it is possible that $x_{B_i}(w)\neq x_{B_{i-1}}(w)$.  This is common to both labelling schemes and does not affect the upcoming discussion. The important thing for what comes next is the definition of $x_{B_i}(w)=\lca_{B_i}(f(w)\cap S^+_i)$}  As a result of this, the transition code $\tau(w,i-1)$ used for the B-trees in \cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved} is shorter (it has length $O(\sqrt{\log n})$ rather than the $O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})$ required by bulk trees) and, on a computer with $O(\log n)$-bit words, the translation from $\sigma^+((w,i-1))$ to $\sigma^+((w,i))$ can be done in constant time, resulting in a constant-time adjacency testing procedure.  (All of the other adjacency-testing operations for both schemes can be done in constant-time.)

  Except for the new definition of the basic signature $\sigma$ and the transition function $\tau$, every other aspect of the resulting labelling scheme and adjacency testing procedure is as described in \cite{dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency}.

  \paragraph{A weighted-labelling scheme:}
  We now describe the changes to \cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved,dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} needed to prove \cref{gk_labelling}.  \Cref{gk_labelling} involves two graphs $G^+$ and $G$ with $G\subseteq G^+\subseteq H\boxtimes P$ and a weight function $\omega:V(G^+)\to\N$.  First, we observe that the final verification performed by the adjacency tester before confirming the presence of an edge is to check a bit that is explicitly stored to indicate the presence (or not) of this edge in $G$.  Thus, the adjacency labelling for the supergraph $G^+$ of $G$ can be made into an adjacency labelling for $G$ by changing a single bit in each label.  Thus, from this point on we assume that $G=G^+$.

  We proceed exactly as in the proof of \cref{shannon_fano_elias_tree}.
  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$ and each $v\in (V(H)\times\{i\})\cap V(G)$, define
  \[
     S_{\omega,i}[v]:=\{(x_f(v),1),\,\ldots,\, (x_f(v),\omega((v,i))\}
  \]
  and define $S_{\omega,i}:=\bigcup_{v\in V(H_i^+)}S_{\omega,i}[v]$.  For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$, treat $S_{\omega,i}$ as a totally ordered set where order is determined by lexicographic comparison.  Note that $|S_{\omega,i}|=\omega(G[V(H)\times\{i-1,i,i+1\}])$ for each $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$ and $\sum_{i=1}^h |S_{\omega,i}|\le 3\omega(G)$.

  We use the convention that a real interval $(a,b)$ contains $(x,j)\in S^+_i$ if (the real interval) $(a,b)$ contains (the real number) $x$. In particular, for a vertex $v\in H$ and $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$, $f(v)\cap S^+_i:=\{(x,j)\in S^+_i:x\in f(v)\}$. Then, just as in the unweighted case $x_{B_i}(v)=\lca_{B_i}(f(v)\cap S^+_i)$.  The only difference at this point is that we apply the machinery in \cite{alstrup.rauhe:improved,dujmovic.esperet.ea:adjacency} beginning with the sets $S_{\omega,1},\ldots,S_{\omega,h}$.  This results in sets $S^+_{\omega,1},\ldots,S^+_{\omega,h}$ of total size $\sum_{i=1}^h |S^+_{\omega,i}|\in O(\omega(G))$.

  This works because a B-tree of height $h$ has at most $6a^h$ leaves.
  The node $\lca_{B_i}(S_{\omega,i}[v])$ is the root of a subtree of $B_i$ with at least $|S_{\omega,i}[v]|=\omega((v,i))$ leaves.  Therefore,
  \begin{align*}
     \depth_{B_i}(x_{B_i}(v))
     & =
     \depth_{B_i}(\lca_{B_i}(\lca(f(v)\cap S_{\omega,i}) \\
     & \le
     \depth_{B_i}(\lca_{B_i}(\lca(S_{\omega,i}[v])) \\
     & \le\height(B_i)-\log_a\omega((v,i))+1 \\
     & \le \log_a|S^+_i| - \log_a\omega((v,i)) + 2 \enspace .
  \end{align*}
  Then
  \begin{align*}
     |\lambda_2((v,i))|
     & \le \lceil \log(12a)\rceil \depth_{B_i}(x_{B_i}(v)) \\
     & \le \log |S^+_{\omega,i}|-\log \omega((v,i)) + O(\log_a|S^+_{\omega,i}|+ \log a) \\
     & \le \log |S^+_{\omega,i}|-\log \omega((v,i)) + O(\sqrt{\log n})
  \end{align*}
  and
  \begin{align*}
     |\lambda_1((v,i))|+|\lambda_2((v,i))|
     & \le \log\omega(G) - \log |S^+_{\omega,i}| + O(\log\log n) \\
     & \quad {} + \log |S^+_{\omega,i}|-\log \omega((v,i)) + O(\sqrt{\log n}) \\
     & = \log\omega(G) - \log\omega((v,i)) + O(\sqrt{\log n}) \enspace .
  \end{align*}

  \paragraph{Clique labelling and membership testing:}

  In the preceding, we have argued that the lengths of the adjacency labels promised in \cref{gk_labelling} are achievable.  All that remains is describe the clique labels and membership testing procedure.

  Let $K$ be a clique in $G=G^+$.  Then $K\subseteq R_i$ for some $i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}$.  Let $X_K:=\{v: (v,i)\in V(K)\}$.  Then $H[X_K]$ is a clique and there exists $v^*\in X_K$ such that $\{v^*\}\cup N^+_H(v^*)\supseteq X_K$.  Therefore, there exists $(v^*,i)\in V(K)$ such that $X_{v^*,i}\supseteq V(K)$. Recall that $\sigma^+((v^*,i))$ and $\pi((v^*,i))$ contain enough information to recover $\sigma((w,j))$ and $\varphi(w)$ and $j-i$ for each $(w,j)\in X_{v^*,i}$.

  \begin{align*}
     |\lambda(K)|
     & = |\lambda(v^*,i)| + O(k) \\
     & \le \log\omega(G) - \log\omega((v^*,i)) + O(\sqrt{\log n}) \\
     & \le \log\omega(G) - \log\min\{\omega((w,j)):(w,j)\in V(K)\} + O(\sqrt{\log n})  \enspace .
  \end{align*}

  Given $\lambda(K)$, $\lambda((w,j))$, and an integer $i\in\{1,\ldots,|V(K)|\}$, the membership tester computes the minimum integer $\ell$ such that $\sum_{j=1}^\ell b_j(K)=i$.  Then the algorithm proceeds exactly like the adjacency testing algorithm to determine if $(w,j)=(w_\ell,j_\ell)$.
